Services / Information visualization

Information visualization

Figures, dashboards and maps for teams that need to see what their data says, and show it to someone else.

Discuss your project

The question we start with

Who needs to see this, and what should they be able to decide?

I start with the reader and the decision, then the data. Together we agree what each view must answer, what can go, and how uncertainty will show.

The rules are Edward Tufte’s: show the data, spend ink on information rather than decoration, put comparisons side by side, and label things where they are, not in a distant legend.

What I make

Figures for papers, posters and grants

Multi-panel figures that match the results text, in vector formats, with colorblind-safe palettes. Built in Altair, matplotlib, Plotly or ggplot2, so you can rebuild them when the data changes.

Dashboards and interactive tools

Single-page dashboards that need no server: maps, filters and linked views that open on a phone and keep working after handover.

Maps and spatial data

County and regional maps, field data and remote-sensing layers, with honest legends and uncertainty shown, not hidden.

Graphics for non-specialists

One-page briefs and explainers for funders, executives, clinicians and patients, in plain language.

What you receive

  • A short brief: the audience, their questions, and the views that answer them
  • The agreed figures or dashboard, with the data and code to rebuild them
  • Notes on limits: what each view can and can’t show
  • Handover documentation your team can follow

The exact deliverables, fee and milestones are set in a written scope before work starts.

Send one figure or a draft dashboard. I’ll return a short critique and a sketch of how I would redraw it. Sometimes that is all you need.
